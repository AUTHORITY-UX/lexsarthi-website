    <h2>Overview</h2>
    <p>On 6 June 2026, the Supreme Court’s AI Committee published the draft <strong>“Regulations for Use of Artificial Intelligence in Courts, 2026”</strong>. The document establishes a comprehensive framework for AI governance in the judiciary, grounded in human primacy, transparency, accountability, and data protection.</p>
    <p>LexSarthi, as India’s first AI‑native law firm, has analysed the draft and submitted formal comments. Below is a one‑page summary of the key provisions, our observations, and actionable recommendations.</p>

    <div class="highlight">
        <strong>📅 Deadline for public comments:</strong> 20 June 2026. <br>
        <strong>📧 Submit to:</strong> office.regcc@sei.nic.in
    </div>

    <h2>Key Principles (Chapter II)</h2>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Human primacy (Reg. 4):</strong> AI must remain subservient to judicial authority – no autonomous decision‑making.</li>
        <li><strong>Transparency & explainability (Reg. 7):</strong> Every AI system must be capable of being understood and explained.</li>
        <li><strong>Accountability (Reg. 8):</strong> Judicial officers remain fully responsible for AI‑assisted outputs; “black box” or hallucination is not a defence.</li>
        <li><strong>Data protection (Reg. 10):</strong> DPDP Act principles apply; sensitive judicial data must be protected.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Permissible & Prohibited Uses (Chapter III)</h2>
    <p><strong>Permissible:</strong> case management, transcription, translation, legal research, administrative automation, accessibility tools, anonymisation, etc. (Reg. 19).</p>
    <p><strong>Absolutely prohibited (Reg. 20):</strong></p>
    <ul>
        <li>❌ Risk scoring (bail, recidivism, flight risk)</li>
        <li>❌ Any form of adjudication or sentencing without human‑in‑the‑loop</li>
        <li>❌ Undisclosed, opaque black‑box AI affecting rights</li>
        <li>❌ Predictive profiling of behaviour</li>
        <li>❌ Surveillance of court participants</li>
        <li>❌ AI‑generated evidence without disclosure</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Institutional Mechanism (Chapter IV)</h2>
    <p>The regulations create an <strong>Apex Body</strong> (Supreme Court) and <strong>AI Committees</strong> in every High Court, supported by an <strong>AI Secretariat</strong> and a <strong>Centre of Research and Excellence (CoRE‑AI)</strong>. This structure is well‑designed for centralised policy‑making and local implementation.</p>

    <h2>Oversight & Audits (Chapter V)</h2>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>AI Register (Reg. 37):</strong> Every court must maintain a register of all approved AI systems.</li>
        <li><strong>Technical & Ethical Impact Assessment (Reg. 35):</strong> Required before approval.</li>
        <li><strong>Controlled Environment Testing (Reg. 36):</strong> Isolated sandboxes for evaluation.</li>
        <li><strong>Annual audits (Reg. 38):</strong> Mandatory every year.</li>
        <li><strong>AI Incident Database (Reg. 39):</strong> Centralised recording of errors and failures.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Transparency & Disclosure (Reg. 43)</h2>
    <p>Any AI‑assisted submission to a court must be disclosed using prescribed forms (Annexure I & II). Non‑disclosure may lead to adverse orders. LexSarthi supports this but recommends that standard forms be finalised before notification and made available in vernacular languages.</p>

    <h2>LexSarthi’s Key Recommendations</h2>
    <div class="highlight">
        <ol>
            <li><strong>Standardise AI Register format:</strong> Provide an open‑source reference implementation for courts with limited IT resources.</li>
            <li><strong>Clarify “in‑house” audits (Reg. 38):</strong> Permit empanelled auditors and containerised audit scripts that run on‑premises without data transfer.</li>
            <li><strong>Allow anonymised real data for testing (Reg. 36):</strong> Subject to data protection impact assessment.</li>
            <li><strong>Interoperable AI Incident Database (Reg. 39):</strong> Common API schema for secure data sharing among High Courts.</li>
            <li><strong>Explicitly approve online, self‑paced training modules (Reg. 49):</strong> Necessary for scaling capacity building.</li>
            <li><strong>Grace period for non‑compliant legacy systems (Reg. 41):</strong> Up to 6 months with heightened oversight.</li>
            <li><strong>Industry collaboration for CoRE‑AI (Reg. 32):</strong> Form an Industry Advisory Group.</li>
        </ol>
    </div>
